PART 1 — THE SYSTEM AT ITS MOST BASIC THEORETICAL LEVEL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

## What it is
A translation machine: it converts editorial intent into produced media. A human decides
what is true and what the angle is (the brief); the machine turns that into a watchable
video. One artifact carries everything between stages: the spec — the contract and the
single source of truth.

## The three layers
1. TRUTH — the brief. What's real, what's the angle. Human-owned; the brief owns truth
   because there is no live grounding.
2. COMPOSITION — the Creative Director → the spec. Angle, segments, rhythm, the three
   channels (text / voice / footage), timing. Where quality is decided; cheapest to upgrade.
3. EXECUTION — research + render. Turns the spec into footage, voice, pixels. Mechanical.
   *The first validation showed this layer is the binding constraint on quality: the
   writing is good, the footage is where it falls apart.*

## The contract, gates, spine, loop
- CONTRACT gate: schema validation right after the CD; rejects/repairs, logs.
- EDITORIAL gate (pre-render critic): is the plan good? Hook, arc, rhythm, script-fit. REVISE → CD.
- OUTPUT gate (post-render QA, vision): did execution match the plan? Real footage vs gradient,
  footage matches the claim, legibility. Flag-and-stop.
- OBSERVABILITY spine: run record + cost meter; every fallback logged loudly.
- CONTROL loop: bounded regeneration, two passes then flag-and-stop.
Principle: review each failure at the layer where it is visible.

## The master clock (deepest structural choice)
TODAY the visual timeline is master and the voice is forced to fit it (truncation bug,
now caught pre-render at Step 5). TARGET is voice-as-master: the narration's measured
length defines the video; segments are timed to the read. Makes truncation impossible and
enables sync. Step 12.

## The principles
Slop is the centroid (pull off with brief, exemplars, constraints) · spend where quality
is decided · measure before optimising · composition over segmentation · register fixed,
structure flexes · format follows function, every segment has a reason · facts are the
human's job · review at the right layer · make silent failures loud · flag-and-stop.

## Phase B — the thin slice (continuing)

### Step 6 — Make Output-QA honest, then fix the visual layer
6a) FRAME-DRIFT FIX (in progress): subtract i × CROSSFADE_DURATION from each extraction
    timestamp so the QA judges the right frames. Makes the gate trustworthy.
6b) HERO-CLIP COMPOSITION (the quality lever toward 4/5), in the CD prompt:
    - Default to ONE hero clip as the visual spine — the longest, best, most on-story
      footage, likely to exist as a long continuous piece — carried across the spine
      segments (same clip_description, sequential moments) with the narration over it.
    - 0–4 supporting clips, only for beats that genuinely need different footage; each
      runs as long as it adds context, then cuts.
    - Gradient discipline: gradients ONLY for deliberate suspense or a pure-data beat.
      Never segment 1 (it is the thumbnail). Never as a silent footage-not-found fallback.
    - Multi-short-clip montage becomes the exception, not the default.
    - Min length: target 35–45s, never under 30s; lean on the hero clip running longer,
      not on padding gradients.
EFFECT: fewer gradient fallbacks (fewer clips to find), a visual spine that tracks the
voice, no repetition-to-fill. Re-run validation and re-score until ~4/5.

### → Keep re-running and watching until quality clears the bar.

## Phase C — make it measurable

### Step 7 — Eval harness + human-anchored judge
Frozen brief set; generate at fixed temperature, N runs each; score with the critic rubric
as judge against your own ratings and the v1 baseline.

### Step 8 — Regression guard
Editing the CD prompt auto-reruns the eval and reports pass or regress.

## Phase D — inputs & coherence

### Step 9 — Brief contract + draft mode
Reject thin briefs with specifics; a cheap brief → CD → critic path with no render.

### Step 10 — Footage hit-rate (the deeper root) + music bed
Better/parallel clip queries, a preflight footage check (fail fast on a no-footage lead),
and an explicit fallback strategy. A hero-clip miss is flagged loudly, never silently
gradiented. Config-driven music (scheme → mood), ducked under VO, dropped under native.
NOTE: the hero-clip model (Step 6) only reduces the hit-rate burden; this step addresses
the root that the validation proved is the real bottleneck.

## Phase E — composition depth & throughput

### Step 11 — Wire transitions + audio_mode into the renderer
Make transition (cut/crossfade/zoom) and audio_mode (vo/native) actually drive the render,
and play a hero clip continuously across its spine segments rather than restarting it.

### Step 12 — Voice-as-master timing (the master-clock fix)
Measure the TTS duration; segment durations become relative weights/minimums; the renderer
lays segments against the measured voice and times cuts to the read; add sync intent.
ABSORBS the old render safety net — truncation becomes impossible by construction.

### Step 13 — Batch runner + review surface
Queue runner + one review surface (passed and flagged videos with frames and reasons).
PREREQUISITE: move run-record and cost-meter off module globals to per-run context before
anything runs in parallel.

## Removed / changed
- The standalone "render safety net" step is gone: Step 5 catches the common truncation
  case and Step 12 dissolves it by construction. Revisit only if a residual minor cut
  appears in practice.
